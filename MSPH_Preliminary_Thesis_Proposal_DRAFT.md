# Liberty University – Department of Public and Community Health
## Preliminary Master's Thesis Research Proposal (EPDM 690) — DRAFT

**Proposed Title:** Campus Support Service Use and Psychological Distress Among University Students

---

## Purpose Statement for Proposed Study

University students report high and rising levels of psychological distress, yet many who could benefit from support never use it. National data show that mental health problems are common among college students and that a large share of students with symptoms receive no treatment or support (1,2,4). Service use has increased over the past decade (3), but gaps persist by subgroup, including by race/ethnicity, student level, and enrollment context (4,13). This body of work belongs to the broader research program on **help-seeking and health service utilization in higher education**.

Key developments in this program include: (a) early population-level studies documenting the prevalence of distress and the treatment gap (1,2); (b) trend studies showing rising service use and persistent disparities (3,4); and (c) a theoretical shift toward explaining *who* uses services, using frameworks such as Andersen's Behavioral Model, in which use is shaped by predisposing factors, enabling factors (e.g., awareness, access), and need (5). Most of this literature centers on counseling and mental health care alone. Less is known about how distress, awareness, and perceived need relate to use of the *full range* of campus supports (counseling, student health, career, and academic support), which students often use together and which may serve as alternative entry points to care.

This study will contribute by examining these relationships in a single university population, including both undergraduate and graduate students, using a standardized distress measure (the Kessler K6) (6,7). It will also add open-ended student feedback on experiences and suggestions. Findings may help campus staff target outreach and plan student support services.

---

## Proposed Research Question(s)

**Population:** Undergraduate and graduate students aged 18+ enrolled at Liberty University.

### Quantitative

**RQ1.** Is the level of psychological distress (K6 score) associated with use of any campus support service in the past 12 months among Liberty University students?
- H0: There is no association between psychological distress and use of campus support services.
- Ha: There is an association between psychological distress and use of campus support services.

**RQ2.** After accounting for distress, are awareness of services and perceived need for support associated with use of each service type (counseling, student health, career, academic support)?
- H0: Awareness and perceived need are not associated with use of each service type, after adjusting for distress and covariates.
- Ha: Awareness and/or perceived need are associated with use of each service type, after adjusting for distress and covariates.

**RQ3.** Does the association between psychological distress and service use differ by student level (undergraduate vs. graduate)?
- H0: The distress–service use association does not differ by student level.
- Ha: The distress–service use association differs by student level.

### Qualitative (supplementary)

**RQ4.** What experiences, barriers, and suggestions do students describe regarding campus support services?

## Proposed Research Methods

**Design.** Cross-sectional, anonymous online survey. The design is quantitative, with a supplementary qualitative component (open-ended items) analyzed separately and integrated at interpretation (convergent mixed-methods, with the qualitative strand secondary) (9).

**Participants and recruitment.** Liberty University students aged 18+ (undergraduate and graduate; residential and online), recruited via university email/listserv, course announcements, and social media, with permission from the relevant offices. Convenience sampling, so findings are not strictly generalizable. Target n ≈ 300–400 (see sample size below).

**Measures** (draft; to finalize with Chair):
- *Psychological distress:* Kessler K6 (6 items, scored 0–24; cutoffs for moderate/serious distress) (6,7).
- *Service use:* Past-12-month use of each of four services (counseling, student health, career, academic support), plus frequency.
- *Awareness:* Whether the student knew each service existed and how to access it.
- *Perceived need:* Whether the student felt they needed support in each area.
- *Covariates (predisposing/enabling):* age, gender, race/ethnicity, student level, residential vs. online enrollment, international status, first-generation status, employment, financial strain (guided by 5).
- *Open-ended items (2–3):* experiences using or not using services, barriers, suggestions.

**Sample size.** Logistic regression with approximately 8–10 predictors needs roughly 10 outcome events per predictor (12), so 100 service users at minimum. Power analysis will be done in G*Power (10). A target of ~300–400 allows for service use rates under 50% and incomplete responses.

**Analysis.**
- Descriptives: frequencies, means/SD, K6 distribution, service use by type.
- RQ1–RQ2: Chi-square/t-tests for bivariate screening, then multivariable logistic regression (any use; each service type) with odds ratios and 95% CIs (11).
- RQ3: Interaction term (distress × student level), with stratified models if significant.
- RQ4: Reflexive thematic analysis of open-ended responses (8), with coding by the student and a second coder.
- Software: SPSS, SAS, or R (to confirm). Missing data handled by reporting and, if needed, multiple imputation. Alpha = 0.05.

**Ethics/IRB.** Liberty University IRB review is required. Anonymous, minimal-risk survey of adults, likely eligible for exempt review. Informed consent on the first page; no identifiers collected. Because distress is assessed, the survey will provide crisis resources (e.g., 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, campus counseling) to all participants. The K6 is a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

**Challenges and costs.** Low response rates; self-selection and social desirability bias; self-reported use; cross-sectional design prevents causal or temporal inference (distress may precede or follow service use). Costs are minimal (free survey platform such as Qualtrics via the university, K6 is free to use). Access to the student email list may require approval.
